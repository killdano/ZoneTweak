ZONETWEAK — for Pimax Lighthouse
In-headset editor for the Pimax safety zone (green boundary) (C:\ProgramData\Pimax\runtime\chaperone_info*.vrchap).
Runs on OpenXR, so it works with whichever OpenXR runtime is active: Pimax's own or SteamVR.

RUN
  Start Pimax Play / Evo with the headset on, then run ZoneTweak.exe once from Windows.
  The first run adds ZoneTweak to Pimax's app library, so after that you can launch it
  from inside VR like any other Pimax app.
  Keep openxr_loader.dll next to it. A desktop window mirrors the left eye.
  Problems go to zonetweak.log in this folder.

CONTROLS (either hand)
  Trackpad press (Index) ....... switch tool: FLOOR beam <-> LASER pointer (with transform)
    (Touch: squeeze; Vive: use the POINTER menu button)
  Trigger near a generator ..... lock the tractor beam on it and move it
    (in FLOOR mode it grabs anything within ~45 cm, no need to stand on it;
     in LASER mode aim anywhere on the shaft, top to bottom, and drag it at that distance)
  Trigger near a wall .......... drop a new generator there and move it
  A on a point ................. delete it
  Thumbstick click ............. undo
  B ............................ open / close the menu
  Menu: point the controller tip into a button and pull the trigger.
  The beam's floor ring is where it acts; the faint big ring is the grab range.

NEW OUTLINE (menu)
  Walk to each real corner, hold the controller over it, pull the trigger.
  Pull the trigger near the first corner to close. A removes the last corner,
  thumbstick click cancels.

MENU
  Point at any button (tip or laser) and a description appears right beside it.
  SAVE ........... writes the boundary into the Pimax files and SteamVR's copies of the same
                   universe (so a Pimax resync can't bring the old one back). The first save
                   keeps a timestamped .bak of each file. Restart Pimax to see the new border.
                   If it says access denied, run the exe as administrator.
  REVERT ......... back to the last saved boundary.
  SQUARE UP ...... straight walls at right angles, lined up with the room.
  SIMPLIFY ....... merges points within 8 cm of a straight line.
  SNAP ........... off by default. On, dragged points pull square to their neighbors.
  SAVED SPACES ... your named play spaces (see below).
  FIX FLOOR ...... if ZoneTweak's grid isn't on your real floor (Pimax sometimes starts
                   the app with the floor offset), press it, rest a controller on the
                   floor during the 3 s countdown. View only, nothing is saved.
  POINTER ........ same as the trackpad press.
                   FLOOR = beam straight down (default). LASER = a pointing beam. Either way
                   only one hand is active: the one that pulled the trigger last (pull the
                   other trigger to swap; it won't swap while you're holding a generator).
                   Point it at the menu to select buttons. Good for points behind a wall.
                   The laser is smoothed (One Euro filter): slow, careful moves are steady
                   even with a shaky hand, fast moves still follow quickly.
  PLAY AREA ...... on the floor next to the yellow play center there's a padlock. Point at
                   it and pull the trigger to unlock. Then, on the floor:
                   yellow center post = move, curved yellow arrows = drag around to turn,
                   blue edge / corner posts = stretch. Click the padlock again to lock.
                   With it unlocked, the menu shows FIT AREA, CENTER IN ROOM,
                   SQUARE TO ROOM and RESET. Then SAVE.
  ZONE SETTINGS .. how close you get before the safety zone appears (warning distance),
                   in 2 cm / 10 cm steps or back to Pimax's default, and the safety zone
                   on/off switch. Writes %LOCALAPPDATA%\Pimax\runtime\profile.json
                   (backed up first). Restart Pimax if it doesn't change right away.
                   PROXIMITY TEST shows the warning distance as an orange line inside
                   the walls. Drag the line with the trigger to set the distance
                   (saved when you let go). Walk or reach past it and the walls glow red:
                   that's where Pimax's safety zone would pop up.
  REALIGN VIEW ... if the green walls don't line up with your room (for example after a
                   Pimax recenter done before ZoneTweak started), turn this on, hold the
                   trigger and drag / twist the whole outline until it sits on Pimax's own
                   green border or your real walls. Stick click resets. Only changes the
                   view, never the saved boundary. A recenter done while ZoneTweak is
                   running is corrected automatically.
  RESTORE ORIGINAL loads the boundary from the oldest backup (Pimax's own, before any edit
                   from this app). Save to write it back.
  B closes the menu.

SAVED SPACES
  SAVE AS NEW .... name what you see now with the on-panel keyboard.
  Tap a space .... loads it AND writes it to Pimax in one step. It becomes the active space.
  UPDATE ......... overwrite the active space with what you see now.
  DELETE ......... press twice.
  Spaces live as JSON files in the "spaces" folder next to the exe.

SWITCH WITHOUT PUTTING THE HEADSET ON
  ZoneTweak.exe --use Door open
  Make a desktop shortcut per space (Target: "...\ZoneTweak.exe" --use Door open),
  run it, and it offers to restart Pimax for you.

EXIT / RESTART
  SAVE & RESTART PIMAX saves, closes ZoneTweak and restarts the Pimax service (the same
  thing Pimax Play's "Restart service" does) so the new boundary loads.
  EXIT: if you saved anything, a popup offers to restart Pimax for you.
  Zone Settings changes (warning distance, zone on/off, recenter) are also sent to Pimax
  live through its own service, so they may apply without a restart.

UNDO A SAVE
  Close Pimax, delete the edited .vrchap and rename its .bak-YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS copy back.

THE PRISON (LOOK AND SOUND)  - all decoration, nothing here is saved to Pimax
  The cell grows and shrinks with your boundary, but only where you're not looking: the
  walls that need to move wait until they're out of view (each wall on its own), and the
  rest stays exactly as it was. If the boundary's main direction swings round a lot, the
  whole cell re-aligns the next time you look straight down at the deck or up at the ceiling.
  GRAPHICS
    Real-time shader renderer: physically based materials (painted steel with chips and
    rust, brushed chrome, rubber, circuit boards with blinking LEDs, hazard paint, riveted
    wall panels), steel deck floor with standing water that mirrors the room, two ceiling
    lamps with real shadows (one has a fan turning under it), volumetric fog that lights
    up around the lasers and lamps, HDR bloom and filmic tone mapping. Touch a laser wall
    and the sheet reacts: shock rings ripple out, the filaments flare white and arcs crawl
    across it; with the pointer, the hit point drags a flickering trail of interference -
    jittering horizontal scanlines salted with harsh TV-static pixels that hang on a
    moment longer - they never fade, they just turn up less and less until they're gone - with lone stray lines thrown out further away - across the sheet. The laser pointer reaches as far as
    the room, at any height. The haze is thin
    overall, with curling vapour pooling round the generators, slow drifting banks of mist in
    the hall, and steam rolling out of the doorways the robots use. Smoke and steam are lit by
    the lasers and lamps around them.
    Menu > GRAPHICS opens a page where you pick ULTRA, HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW or POTATO directly. It
    switches instantly (a brief flicker while it rebuilds) and is remembered. ULTRA is the
    default (8x anti-aliasing, 4K shadows, full fog). If the headset stutters, try HIGH; LOW
    also turns off the floor reflections and uses fewer lights. POTATO is the bare minimum for
    weak GPUs: no anti-aliasing, no shadows, plain floor (no water), no surface detail, no
    mist or haze patches, very few lights and particles.
    The same page has RENDER SCALE (100/90/80/70%: renders fewer pixels and scales the image
    back up to the headset's size; each step down is roughly 20% less GPU work) and FOVEATED
    (NVIDIA RTX / GTX 16 GPUs: the outer part of each eye is shaded at a lower rate, where the
    lens blurs it anyway; the middle stays full quality). The header shows live timings:
    total GPU and CPU milliseconds per frame and how long each pass takes (shadows,
    reflection, depth prepass, scene, fog, post). They're also written to the log every
    10 seconds.
    Always on, no setting: nothing is drawn in the parts of the image hidden behind the lens
    edge (when the headset runtime reports them), the floor reflection renders at reduced
    resolution, everything opaque is laid down depth-first so each visible pixel is only
    lit once, and room pieces outside the view aren't drawn.
  LASER WALLS
    Faint energy sheets with uneven filaments, occasional brown-outs, and rare random
    overcharge surges (roughly once a minute per wall): a flare at the base, then a bolt
    that accelerates across the wall with a real arc crackle and hits the far generator.
  GENERATORS
    The laser sheet is only projected by each post's energy blade - not the bell cap on top
    and not the base machinery. Below the blade, two diagonal box-section emitters telescope
    out of every generator, angled over its base to just above the deck; the sheet comes out
    of a glowing trench along their top, so it still reaches the floor. They retract into the post when it's torn free or still deploying, and extend
    again once it's planted. Hot points glow along each blade, jetting energy into the sheet,
    and the sheet's edges leave through glowing lenses set in finned emitter heads: one under
    the bell for the top edge, one low on the post for the bottom edge. When the floor
    telescope extends it pushes the bottom lens out with it, so the bottom edge always
    comes out of the lens - at the head when stowed, at the telescope tip on the floor.
    Every base carries four hydraulic legs and two tethers, each in its own slot. When a
    joined neighbour moves, a base turns smoothly to its new heading and any leg or tether
    near the neighbour tucks in toward the shaft - nothing vanishes or jumps. A torn-off
    base (and every piece it's cut into) keeps the same layout.
    Placing a corner fires a capsule that lands, pulses, telescopes up, flickers and lights.
    Deleting one changes the boundary at once, and the old post leaves in one of four ways,
    picked at random: it DECOMMISSIONS (beacon goes red, its walls unzip back into the
    neighbours, the shaft telescopes down and sinks into its base, the pistons fold down and the
    cables unplug and coil up, leaving a clean, powered-down base), gets EXTRACTED (a claw
    drops from the ceiling, clamps it and rips it out through the roof), drops through a
    HATCH (the deck opens, it sinks into the dark and the doors slam shut; the sealed hatch stays in the
    deck until you look away), or OVERLOADS
    (arcs crawl up it, the bell blows off and the shaft crumples). Whatever base or debris
    is left behind gets salvaged by the robots. Undo brings it straight back.
    A new generator dragged out of a wall hovers until you let go; only then does it grow
    its floor mount. Changed your mind? Grab it again while the base is still growing (or
    while it's still re-docking, about 1.4 s) and the half-made base just sinks back in:
    nothing gets torn off. Same for a freshly fired capsule that hasn't finished landing.
    The base's pistons and tethers lock onto a steel slewing collar round the middle of the
    coil housing, so their brackets turn with the base.
    Each sits on a slide-mount base: you can nudge it a little, and if you let go it stays
    where you left it, off-centre on the base. The shaft only travels on the smooth centre
    of the mount: its tethers draw in and go tight as it moves, and at ~11 cm the mount
    bottoms out and holds, the cables humming and sparking; keep pulling another ~11 cm (the controller buzzes harder as it strains) and the shaft
    snaps free with a hard jolt. It floats while you hold it and grows a new base where you
    drop it. Drop it back on its old base (within ~20 cm of the centre) and it re-docks:
    pistons and cables reach up and lock on, leaving it off-centre where it landed. Drop it
    close enough that the new base would overlap the old one, and the new base shears that
    section off: the chunks fly, land, and a Scuttler collects them one at a time before the
    rest of the old base is cut up and removed. Generators close enough for their bases to overlap share one joint
    base (double or triple), which forges itself under them: welding arcs trace its outline,
    the slab swells up glowing hot, steams as it cools and the legs re-route onto it. Pull
    them apart and it retracts. Each can still slide on its own mount and rips off the same way.
    Rip one off a shared base and its torn stump stays part of the slab until a robot starts
    cutting it free; the seam flashes as it separates. A new base planted over several old
    ones shears a chunk out of each of them.
  SALVAGE
    A robot comes for every ripped-off base. Out in the open a big one does it: a Warden
    crouches and runs a circular saw round the rim, or a Tread cuts it with a laser; then it
    grips the base (the Warden with both hands, the Tread with hydraulic clamp arms on its
    bow) and carries it out a doorway in one piece, backing out first so the load never
    swings into a generator. If a big robot
    can't get there without brushing a generator, a small Scuttler comes instead, plasma-
    cuts the base into three pieces, lifts each onto its cargo bed with an electromagnet arm
    and carries them out one per trip. The base smokes,
    more while it's being cut. Cutters unfold before they bite and fold away after, and every
    arm eases between poses (cut, grab, lift, carry) - nothing snaps into place. Robots
    switch off any laser wall they have to pass through.
  ROBOTS
    Wardens (biped, welding torch + claw), Treads (tracked, turret head, welder arm) and
    Scuttlers (small four-wheeled salvage rovers with a plasma cutter and a cargo bed).
    Every robot is different: new and clean, polished chrome, or old and rusted (old ones
    spark at the joints and their eyes flicker). They fix faulty generators, patrol, stop to
    talk to each other in their own language, and sometimes scan you. When you move a
    generator they scatter out of the way. If two meet head-on and can't get past, the one
    with the less important job steps aside and lets the other through. A robot that gets
    wedged against a wall or generator backs off, turns round and goes another way. Walls cut out briefly if a robot gets too close,
    so they never touch the lasers, and they keep their shoulders and arms well clear of the
    walls and generators as they walk past. When a wall is powered down, its blade hot
    points and the floor emitters' jets go dark with it.
  INCIDENT (once per launch)
    Somewhere between 3 and 10 minutes in, one of the wall junction boxes starts sparking. A
    minute later it blows: debris flies, it catches fire and the alarm sounds with red
    beacons spinning overhead. The fire comes in bursts: it dies back under thick black
    smoke, then flares up again a few times, throwing flames and debris. A Warden and a
    Tread run in, unhook their CO2 bottles and take the horns off them (the Warden carries
    its bottle in its claw, the Tread's stays on its hull on a hose), and fight it in bursts:
    a blast of swirling white gas that boils over the fire and fans out across the wall,
    a pause while the flames claw their way back, another blast - until the last one puts
    it out. Then they stow the extinguishers, walk over, unfold their cutting and welding
    heads, weld the box back together, fold the tools away, look it over and leave, and a Scuttler clears the debris. It only ever happens
    once per run.
  BASE STATIONS
    Your real base stations, hung from the ceiling on bio-mechanical arms, read from
    Pimax's lighthouse database. Every 20-60 s one fires a barcode scan sweep.
  SOUND
    Industrial horror bed (sub rumble, groaning metal, distant clangs, steam, drips, far-off
    robot chatter) in a large reverberant hall; transformer hum near the walls; modal metal
    impacts, hydraulics, servos, grinding, arc welding, tearing metal. Distant sounds are
    duller and wetter, and everything is panned to where it happens.
  If you hear nothing, check Windows' default playback device is the headset.
