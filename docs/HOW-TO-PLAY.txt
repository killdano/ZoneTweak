CONTRABAND - the bonus game in ZoneTweak (build 14)
====================================================

Your real room is a prison cell. The green ring is your real boundary, held up by
generators. Wardens pour into the hall outside through four doors and lean on your
ring to drain it. Somewhere on a cart in your room is a half-built EMP. Finish it
by hand and you're out. Let them carry it out a door and you lose.

It's played in the boundary you've just shaped in ZoneTweak, so the walls you dragged
into place are the walls you defend.

START
-----
1. Set up your play space in ZoneTweak (or in Pimax Play / SteamVR) as usual.
2. In ZoneTweak, press B for the menu and pick PLAY CONTRABAND (the big red button
   at the top). The view fades to black and comes back in the game, in the boundary
   you've got (saved or not), with your controllers turned into gloves.
3. Pause menu (B pointing at nothing) > BACK TO ZONETWEAK fades you back to editing,
   with everything as you left it. The results board after a round has it too.
4. A desktop shortcut can go straight into the game: add --play to its Target
   ("C:\Games\ZoneTweak\ZoneTweak.exe" --play).
5. If the gloves don't sit where your real hands are: pause menu > FIT GLOVES. If the
   floor's at the wrong height: pause menu > SET FLOOR (or ZoneTweak's FIX FLOOR -
   it's the same floor). Graphics quality, render scale and foveated rendering are on
   ZoneTweak's GRAPHICS page and apply to the game too.
6. Something wrong? zonetweak.log beside the exe says what happened (the last
   session's is zonetweak-prev.log).
While the app runs it tells Windows you're using the PC, so the monitors don't blank
or go to sleep halfway through a round.

Other headsets (a Quest 2 over Link or Air Link, Steam Link or Virtual Desktop): the
game takes the best boundary it can get. If it's only a rectangle or a guess, TRACE
ROOM on the console: point at the floor and pull the trigger at each corner of your
play area (your headset's own boundary shows you where it is), then point back at the
first corner. B takes a corner back. It's saved for that headset; USE BOUNDARY goes
back to ZoneTweak's boundary.

CONTROLS (Index-style controllers; Vive wands and Touch are mapped too)
--------
  Grip (squeeze)         Grab anything physical: levers, pump handles, cranks,
                         valves, knobs, plugs, fuses, the screwdriver, the wrench,
                         the flashlight, the radio handset, the cart's push handles,
                         leech tethers, the prod, the harpoon's grips, its winch and
                         its line.
  Index finger out       Poke buttons with your fingertip (keypad, Simon, console).
  Trigger half-pull      Laser pointer on (aim). Points at console buttons too.
                         At a loose tool it locks an arc on: flick to pull it over.
  Trigger full pull      Click a button you're pointing at, or press-drag-release
                         on the floor out in the hall to draw a magenta wall.
  Trigger held on a      Take it down: it folds away while you hold (about 1.5 s)
  magenta wall           and unfolds if you let go early. All its points come back.
  Thumb on A or B        Laser on, showing what that button will act on.
  Hold A (1 s)           Supercharge the green span your beam passes through:
                         anything touching it fries for 5 s, then 20 s to recharge.
                         On the harpoon turret: supercharge it (loads a spear too).
  Hold B (1 s)           Supercharge the magenta wall your beam picks out.
                         Let go of A or B early: cancelled, nothing spent.
  B at nothing           Pause menu (resume, restart, fit gloves, set floor,
                         graphics, back to ZoneTweak, quit).
  Thumbstick click       Pause menu too (and the Menu button on a Quest controller).
  (Quest: X and Y on the left hand are A and B. No trigger click on Touch: the last
  bit of the pull is the click.)
  Left wrist             Next wave countdown, points, device %, supercharge.

FIT GLOVES
----------
Open the menu and pick FIT GLOVES. Hold one controller still where it feels natural,
then squeeze the grip of your other hand right next to that glove: the glove comes
with your grabbing hand. Move and turn it until it sits where your real hand is, then
let go. Do the other hand the same way. DONE (or B) finishes, RESET puts both back.
Fitting one glove fits the other as its mirror image, and the laser moves with the
glove. To re-aim a laser, grab the ball on it with your other hand and move it where
you want the laser to point (the other laser mirrors it). The fit is saved for next
time. Your forearms follow from where your head and hands are.

SETUP
-----
- The console in your room: FEWER / MORE generators, and the amber button sets the
  difficulty (EASY / NORMAL / HARD; it stays set between rounds).
- Generators and the ring cost points; what's left builds the maze. Draw magenta
  walls out in the hall so the wardens have to walk further. Too long a detour and
  they lose patience and burn straight through it. When you draw one, a pod flies
  out of your hand to each end and deploys into a base station; the wall lights once
  they're up (it blocks the wardens as soon as you draw it). Hold the trigger on a
  wall to take it down again; you get all its points back.
- This is a good time to look around your room for the screwdriver and the wrench,
  and to spot the prod in its bracket on one of the generators (a blinking amber
  PROD sign over it; which generator changes every round).
- When you're ready, pull the big red LIGHTS OUT lever beside the console toward you
  and down. The hall lights die and the first wave is on its way.

THE SCREWDRIVER AND THE WRENCH
------------------------------
Your hands can't turn screws or bolts. Every round one of the cart's covers - the
access plate on top or the serial compartment's door on its side - is held on by
screws and the other by bolts (which is which changes), and some blown generators have
a scorched cover screwed or bolted on too. The screwdriver and the wrench are somewhere
in your room: on the floor near one of the walls, down by the radio, under the console,
or in a tool clip on a generator panel. Their chrome catches the light every few
seconds, and the flashlight makes them shine. The cart has painted outlines where they
belong (DRIVER and WRENCH); hang them back there and the outline turns green. From
across the room you can pull them to you (trigger half-pull, then flick).
The screwdriver: point its tip at a screw head. The tip is magnetic: it snaps into the
head and stays in until you pull your hand away. Then twist your wrist.
The wrench: bring its box end over a bolt head, the wrench roughly across the bolt, and
it drops on. Then swing the handle round the bolt.
Either way:
  - the wrong way (clockwise) it glows red and shakes: it won't go any tighter;
  - counter-clockwise it glows yellow while it breaks loose, then green as it comes out;
  - there's no ratchet: when your wrist (or your swing) runs out, lift the tool off,
    turn back, set it back on and go again. Wiggling it back and forth does nothing.
Each one rises as it comes out. Stuck without the tool you need? A few seconds into a
step that needs it your contact calls to say where it's lying. Throw one out of your
room and it turns up back inside.

THE ROUND
---------
- THE DEVICE (on the cart): eight hands-on stages, in a different order every round
  (the plate's always before the wires, the cord always last): unscrew or unbolt the
  access plate, plug the three wires into the matching sockets, play a game in the
  side compartment (see THE DEVICE'S GAMES), match the three dials to their marks,
  wind the coil (it leaks the moment you let go), get the compartment door on the
  cart's side open (screwed or bolted shut), find the serial number inside (it's dark
  in there: use the flashlight) and key it in (no ENTER: the right code goes straight
  through), a second game (always a different one), then the engine: pull the cord
  hard - the first two pulls turn it over but it doesn't catch, the third fires it up
  and it shakes the cart and revs - and once it's up to speed (about 4 seconds) push
  the big red button on top. Touch its cap with a fingertip or slap it. Both power
  leads have to be plugged into the back of the cart.
- POWER LEADS: two orange cables run from a POWER box on the floor into the back of
  the cart. When a warden takes the cart (or the cart goes more than about 2.4 m from
  the box) they rip out; once it's back, plug them back in - the sockets blink red
  until you do.
- GENERATORS: wardens leaning on a span drain the two generators that hold it up.
  Below 30% a generator beeps and your contact warns you: pump the handle on its panel
  to top it up (filling it shoves them back), or supercharge the span with A to fry
  them off it. Ignore it and it blows. A blown generator needs the work order on its
  panel done before it comes back (patch cable, crank, fuse, valve, breaker, a
  scorched cover to unscrew or unbolt). Every generator you lose shrinks your room;
  the breaker lever shrinks it on purpose.
- LEECHES: drones fly in along the maze, hop over the beam and hover above a generator
  on a tether clamped to it. Before they bite through (about 4 s) and shut it down:
  grab the tether and yank it off (the leech limps away and comes back in about a
  minute), or hit it hard with the prod's electric tip to kill it for good - tethered
  or flying about. The prod starts in its bracket on one of the generators (a
  different one every round); when a leech clamps on, your contact says where the
  prod is. A hard swing takes down a warden or a heavy too, but the third one snaps
  the prod for the rest of the round.
- IF THE RING SHRINKS OFF THE DEVICE its backup field holds them off for a few
  seconds (FIELD and a countdown): grab a push handle and drag the cart back inside
  your ring. With fewer than three generators your ring is gone: a couple of wardens
  come straight in for the device while the rest crowd round outside your boundary.
- WHEN A WARDEN GRABS THE CART (even out of your hands) it hauls it to the closest
  door, through your maze. Grab a push handle and yank it back hard - a good yank
  gives you 3 seconds of field to drag it home - or harpoon the warden. If it gets the
  cart out the door, you lose. The cart is heavy: drag it, don't fling it.
- THE HARPOON: at 25%, 50% and 75% of the device a turret rises inside your ring.
  Grab both grips, swing it, pull the trigger. Its lamp: GREEN, a full spear; YELLOW,
  reel-only (hooks a robot or the cart, no live wire); dark, empty. Reel a robot in
  with a green spear and it reloads yellow; now and then a load comes yellow from the
  start, with two reel-ins.
    A warden: crank the winch (or grab the line and yank) to haul it in - drag it
    through your green ring or a magenta wall and it fries.
    The device or its carrier: haul it home the same way. Spearing the cart when
    they've got it sets off a little EMP: the warden hauling it lets go and the ones
    round it stumble about for a while.
    The floor or a wall: the line becomes a live wire that fries whatever walks into
    it (swing the gun and the line swings with it). Grab the line: it unhooks and goes
    dead in your hand, and the cleat on the turret's post lights up. Tie it on there
    and it's a live trap (up to three); let go anywhere else and it's gone. Strung
    through a magenta wall, a live line keeps the wall supercharged for a minute.
  TRACTOR BEAM: with no spear loaded and the cart lying on the floor, aim at the cart
  and pull the trigger: the beam hauls it home and nobody can take it out of it.
  SUPERCHARGE IT: point at the turret, hold A until the ring fills (it uses your
  supercharge, and loads a spear if it was spent). A supercharged spear shocks dead
  everything it hits and flies on through.
- THE RADIO: your contact only talks into the handset. When something's waiting the
  lamp blinks and the speaker plays a prompt to pick up. Hold the handset to your ear:
  you get what's waiting, what matters first (old warnings drop off), then the next
  wave - how many of what, and which doors. Leave it lying about and it's back on the
  radio once you look away.

THE DEVICE'S GAMES
------------------
Twice a round the device wants a game, behind the hatch on the cart's left side. Each
time it's one of these (the two are never the same). Botch one and it starts over as
a different game; it never undoes a stage you'd finished. None of them starts its
clock until you take hold of it.
  SIMON        Press START, watch the colours (each has its own tone), repeat them.
               Three rounds, one longer each time. A wrong button starts it over.
  NEEDLE       Take the knob and keep the wandering needle in the green; it kicks
               now and then. Out of the green too long and it starts over.
  FREQUENCY    Two knobs, HOW FAST and HOW TALL, tune your amber wave onto the green
               one, which drifts. While they match it locks and the bar fills; let
               it slip and the bar drains slowly.
  SAFE         Turn the dial and feel for the numbers: the clicks get heavier as you
               near one, and right on it there's a clunk (and the SIGNAL meter
               fills). Stop on it and hold for a second: a lamp lights. Three
               numbers and it's open.
  SWITCHBOARD  A caller lamp (1-4) blinks over its cord, and a line lamp (A-D) blinks
               over the jack it wants: pull that cord's plug and push it into that
               jack. Four calls to put through. Once you've picked up a plug, calls
               ring out if you're slow (the lamps blink faster near the end); two lost
               calls, or plugging into the wrong line twice, starts it over.
  VALVES       Three handwheels, three gauges. Each valve raises its own gauge but
               bleeds the next one, so you have to balance them: all three in the
               green band at once, for three seconds. Leave a gauge in the red for a
               second and a half and it blows (starts over).
  FAR SIDE     The code isn't on the hatch: a screen pops up on the far side of the
               cart, facing away. Walk round, read the five symbols, come back and
               press them in order on the keypad. Press a wrong one and the code
               changes - go round and read it again.

MISFIRES
--------
About half the time the big red button doesn't set it off: the engine coughs, puts out
a weak pulse (the wardens near the cart stagger about for ~15 seconds) and dies. A win
without a misfire makes the next one likelier; after two in a row, it's certain. Then:
  1. The SERVICE hatch on the back half of the cart's top is held down by four screws
     or bolts (which one changes) - take them out.
  2. Grab the handle in its middle and pull it up: the guts come up out of the cart -
     three layers of boards, wires, thick cables, fuses, circuit cards, relays,
     capacitors and jumpers.
  3. Pick up the radio. Your contact tells you the fix - one or two of:
       cut a wire (by colour, or colour and stripe: "the blue and yellow wire");
       cut a thick cable and splice in the patch cable;
       replace a fuse (by colour);
       replace the burnt circuit card, the cracked relay or the bulging capacitor;
       pull out a jumper (by colour).
     Then what each thing looks like and how to do it. Still at it 45 seconds later?
     He calls back with just that part. After you've heard it, the radio's screen
     lists it.
  4. The wire cutters and every spare - a fuse of each colour, a card, a relay, a
     capacitor, the yellow patch cable - are in the tray on the cart's bottom shelf.
     Cutters: hold them, put the jaws on the wire, squeeze the trigger. Parts: pull
     the bad one out, put the spare where it was and let go.
     The patch cable: once the cable's cut, both cut ends spit sparks and glow yellow
     with a ghost of the patch across them (PATCH CABLE HERE), and the patch cable
     in the tray glows too. Pick it up - a line runs from it to the cut - and hold it
     over the cut so it bridges both ends; when it says LET GO, let go and it clamps
     on.
  5. Do anything the fix didn't ask for (the wrong wire, a part that was fine, a
     wrong-coloured fuse) and the guts sink back into the cart (RESETTING...). Inside,
     everything gets shuffled - different parts, a different fix. Pull the handle back
     up and pick up the radio again.
  6. Done: push the handle back down, start the engine (it's warm: one good pull) and
     push the button when it lights.
Now and then (about 1 in 5) it misfires a second time: there are two new things to
fix. Never a third.

TUNING (optional)
-----------------
Every number in the rules can be changed without a new build: copy
contraband-tuning.sample.json to contraband-tuning.json beside the exe and edit it (you
can delete the lines you don't change). The handiest ones:
  pressure     how many robots every wave sends (difficulty multiplies this)
  drain        how fast wardens leaning on your ring drain a generator
  waveEvery    seconds between waves        firstWave  seconds to the first one
  superCool    seconds before you can supercharge again
  leechBite    seconds before a clamped leech shuts its generator down
  fieldSecs    seconds the device's backup field holds once it's exposed
  pool         points for generators, ring and maze
  maxRobots    walkers and heavies in the hall at once
If the file has a typo it's ignored (zonetweak.log says why).

KNOWN ROUGH EDGES
-----------------
Balance still comes from a simulated player, not real rounds. In a 183 sq ft room it
wins most rounds on EASY, about half on NORMAL and a third on HARD (it
doesn't use the supercharged harpoon, the tractor beam, the prod or the force pull).
What felt wrong, slow, confusing or great? Open an issue.

CHANGES
=======

BUILD 14
--------
- Contraband lives inside ZoneTweak now: PLAY CONTRABAND at the top of ZoneTweak's menu
  fades to black and into the game (the controllers become gloves); the pause menu's
  BACK TO ZONETWEAK fades back. ZoneTweak's own room stays as it was: just your
  generators. One app, one log (zonetweak.log), one floor, one set of graphics settings.
- Five new device games: FREQUENCY, SAFE, SWITCHBOARD, VALVES and FAR SIDE (see THE
  DEVICE'S GAMES). A round's two game stages are always different games.
- The prod starts in a bracket on one of the generators (which one changes every
  round, and with how many generators you have), under a blinking PROD sign. The
  leech warning ends with where the prod is.
- Misfire: a wrong move sinks the guts back into the cart before they get shuffled.
- Misfire: the patch cable shows where it goes (see MISFIRES, step 4).
- Supercharge recharges 25% slower (20 s, was 15).
- Windows no longer blanks the monitors or sleeps while you play.
- ZoneTweak has FIT TOOL (on its menu), the same idea as FIT GLOVES: grab the tool with
  your other hand and move it where it sits right in your hand.
- The floor fixes itself when it starts too high, too (you're "really short" after
  picking the headset up off the desk), not just too low.
- Touch: the trigger clicks at 97% of its pull.

BUILD 13
--------
- The last stage is a pull-start engine and a big red button (three pulls cold, one
  warm).
- Your contact calls to say where the screwdriver or the wrench is lying.
- Dropped tools and parts rest on top of things instead of sinking into them.
- No magenta wall starts from the floor inside your room.
- One AGAIN button after a round; the maze never carries over.
- Quest and other headsets: the best boundary the runtime gives, TRACE ROOM for the
  real shape.
- The misfire call says what each thing looks like; he calls back if you're stuck.
- The game follows your room when the headset re-centres.
- Radio lines say what matters first.

BUILD 12
--------
- Misfires (see MISFIRES). EASY sends fewer robots (x0.55 of NORMAL).
- On a Pimax through SteamVR the game keeps your Pimax boundary (SteamVR's is a 1 m
  placeholder there).

BUILD 11
--------
- The harpoon's green and yellow spears; stages shuffled every round; power leads; the
  wrench seats at a natural angle.

BUILD 10
--------
- Robots have power: supercharges and draining your ring wear them down. Spearing the
  cart sets off a little EMP. Harpoon lines tie off on the turret's cleat. The wrench.
  No wiggling screws out. Better button presses. Simon tones. Floor too high? Fixed
  automatically, or SET FLOOR.
